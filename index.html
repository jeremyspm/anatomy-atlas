What is this?

A 3D body you can rotate, peel apart one system at a time, and tap to name any structure. It is an atlas, not a quiz — nothing here is marked, scored, or fed into your study schedule.

How to use it

  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom.
  • Tick a system to load it. Tick more than one and they overlay in the same body — the one you tapped last stays solid and the rest dim, so you can see how they sit together.
  • Tap a structure to see its name and which layer it belongs to.
  • “only” isolates one sub-layer and frames it. Ghost hidden layers keeps what you switched off as a faint shell for context.

Two kinds of model

Body systems share one coordinate space, so they stack into a single body. Reference detail models are higher-resolution regional scans authored separately — they cannot line up with the body, so opening one closes everything else. That is a property of the source models, not a bug.

What each system actually contains

The models are anatomical exports, not a textbook syllabus, and two of them cover less than their name suggests — so the panel says so rather than letting you find out by hunting for something that was never in the file:

  • Integumentary is hair and nails. The source has no skin mesh, so there is no skin layer to switch on.
  • Reproductive is the male set. The source body is male and has no female reproductive organs.
  • Lymphatic is nodes plus spleen, thymus and tonsils — there are no lymphatic vessels or ducts in the export, so no vessel layer is offered.
  • Muscle attachments are the origin (red) and insertion (blue) footprints painted on bone — switch Skeletal on with it.
  • Skeletal (a body system, overlays everything) and Skeleton (full) (reference detail, opens on its own) are two different source models, not a duplicate.

Downloads & data

Each system is a separate file, listed with its size, and only downloads when you switch it on — nothing loads by itself on a phone. After the first load your browser caches it. There is no account, no tracking, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Where the models come from (admin note)

Meshes are Z-Anatomy and AnatomyTOOL / Open3DModel, used under CC BY-SA 4.0 — the credit line in the panel is the licence requirement, so it must not be removed. anatomy-systems.js is copied byte-for-byte from Giga and is not edited here; anatomy-systems-extra.js sits on top of it and promotes the eleven systems Giga exported but never wired into its registry, each with a layer scheme authored against the real export. Both are checked by .build/validate.js, which fails if the copy drifts or if a scheme stops matching its model. Structure names are the source models' own (Terminologia Anatomica), not rewritten.
